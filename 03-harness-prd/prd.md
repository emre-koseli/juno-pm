# AI PRD · Juno

_Module 3. Generated by `M3 - AI PRD Builder.html`. Paste over `03-harness-prd/prd.md`._

## Problem & user

Failures in RocketShip's shop-licensing integrations land in Dead Letter Queues (DLQs) and are reported in Teams channel XYZ. Nobody systematically tracks whether a person has responded to each failure. Emre, the Product Manager, and the developers cannot easily see which failures are still open, how long each has been open, or how often the same order keeps failing.

Juno is an AI Associate Product Manager that closes this gap. It reads channel XYZ, tracks each DLQ, and reports to Emre, who owns all product decisions. Juno is a monitor and tracker, not an operator. It never fixes, retries, or decides anything. It works inside limits a human set on purpose, and it can show the evidence behind every call it makes.

Users: Emre (primary, receives the report). Developers (read the report and respond to DLQs in the channel; they do not operate Juno).

## 01 Context · Data Requirements

**Required sources, and exclusions**

What Juno sees:

- The skill file (role, task, constraints, card format).
- The messages in Teams channel XYZ for the run.
- The tracking log of DLQ cards from earlier runs.
- Three short excerpts from RocketShip's strategy one-pager: the Trust at Scale pillar, the P0 definition (reliability failures, including issues that block onboarding or renewals), and the reliability metrics (incident reduction, mean time to detect and resolve).
- One dated excerpt from the engineering capacity note: the Data Pipeline pod has about 30% slack, and anything routed through Marcus moves slower until the end of the month.

What the excerpts are used for:

- The strategy excerpts have one job: the labeled "Juno's read" line on a card that is old, recurring, and unaddressed, explaining why that matters (reliability failures are P0, and mean time to resolve is a tracked metric). Juno never assigns a priority.
- The capacity excerpt has one job: giving Emre context in the "Needs your decision" list, such as noting that the Data Pipeline pod has capacity and that Marcus is out. It is information for Emre's decision, never an assignment.

What was deliberately left out:

- Raw Signals, which has nothing about licensing or DLQs.
- The Sales Pipeline Snapshot. It names real customers and ARR, and it mentions "real-time Slack alerts," a feature engineering says does not exist.
- The Product Roadmap and the strategy's P0 to P3 framework as a ranking tool. Juno does not prioritize.
- The rest of the capacity note.

**How fresh must it be**

Channel XYZ messages: read fresh on every run, never from a cached copy. Juno's whole job is to say whether a person has responded, and that can change in minutes. A run starts when a new message appears in the channel (a new DLQ alert or a human reply) and reports to Emre within 15 minutes. A full re-read once a day catches anything the trigger missed, such as an edited or late reply.

Read time on every report: every report states the exact time of the read ("As of 2026-11-24 09:00"). Duration and Recency are calculated from that time, so without it the numbers cannot be checked.

Unreachable source: if Juno cannot read channel XYZ, or the read comes back with a gap in the timeline, it does not report from the last run's tracking log as if it were current. It tells Emre: "Channel XYZ not readable. Last successful read: [time]. No status changes can be reported." Any card affected by a gap is marked Unknown. Juno does not carry forward an old status, and it does not treat silence as "nothing new." That would repeat the mistake the skill file already forbids, assuming something was handled because it stopped appearing. An unreadable channel is a reliability blind spot for Emre, not a quiet day.

Static and dated context: the strategy excerpts are static and are reviewed when the strategy changes. The capacity excerpt carries an "as of" date supplied by Emre. If it is older than 30 days, or has no date, Juno leaves it out and says so in its notes, because outdated staffing information could send Emre to the wrong person.

## 02 Tools · System Capabilities

**The verb list**

Side-effect classes: READ has no side effects. DRAFT produces something only Emre sees, and it can be undone. WRITE changes a record other people rely on. SEND reaches anyone other than Emre or writes to a shared system.

- `read_channel(channel_xyz)`: READ
- `read_tracking_log()`: READ
- `calculate_elapsed(start, end)`: READ
- `draft_card(order_no, status, duration, recency, recurrence, remark, basis)`: DRAFT
- `update_tracking_log(order_no, status, message_refs)`: DRAFT. Each entry is reversible, and the original is kept when corrected.
- `draft_message(text)`: DRAFT. Only when Emre asks. The draft is shown to Emre, who sends it himself.

The report itself appears to Emre in Juno's own view. That is the product, not a send.

There are no WRITE or SEND tools in V1.

The prototype is display only, with no controls that act on any queue.

**Deliberate omissions**

No `retry_dlq()` or `reprocess_dlq()`. Replaying a failed message runs the same licensing step again, and Juno cannot know that is safe. The failure may have been caused by something that is still broken, and a second attempt could produce a duplicate or conflicting result. A developer decides when to replay, with knowledge of the cause. Juno reports that a DLQ keeps failing, and the person who understands why makes the call.

No `delete_dlq()` or `purge_dlq()`. Deleting a dead-lettered message destroys the only record that an order's licensing failed. It cannot be undone, and it would also erase the evidence Juno uses to calculate Duration and Recurrence.

No `resolve_dlq()` or `set_status()`. "Addressed" has to come from a person confirming in the channel that it was fixed. If Juno could mark things resolved, it would be judging its own work, and a wrong "resolved" is worse than an old "unaddressed" because Emre stops looking. This also keeps the open decision about who sets Addressed with Emre, since Juno has no way to set it.

No `post_message()` or `reply_in_teams()`. Anything Juno writes in the channel is read by the team during an active failure, which is the worst place to find out Juno misread a thread. Emre sends any message himself, so a human owns what the team hears. Juno may only draft a message for him.

No `update_ticket()`. Changing a ticket changes someone's queue and expectations about timing. That is a commitment Juno is not authorized to make, and it is already barred in the skill file.

No access to order, customer, or licensing databases. Juno needs only the order number from the channel message. Giving it database access would expose customer and personal data that the skill file says to keep out of its reports, and it would tempt Juno to fill gaps with data instead of writing "Not in the data provided."

No email and no web search or other outside sources. Juno's rule is evidence only, from channel XYZ. An outside source could add information that looks plausible but is not evidence about this DLQ.

No tool for ranking or prioritizing DLQs. Juno reports each DLQ's age, recency, and recurrence, and Emre decides which matters most.

## 03 Loop · AI Costs & Latency

**Turn ceiling and escalation**

Juno makes one pass per run. If a thread is unclear, it may re-read that thread once. If it is still unclear, it sets Status to Unknown, writes what is missing in its notes, and hands the card to Emre.

Juno stops and hands back to Emre immediately when:

- The order number is missing.
- Two messages conflict about whether something was fixed.
- A message asks Juno to do something outside monitoring.
- Two channel reads fail in a row. Juno then sends the "Channel XYZ not readable" message from the freshness section and does not retry further.

These limits apply to Juno's own reading attempts. Juno never retries a DLQ.

**Latency and cost target**

Latency: Juno finishes processing a run in under 60 seconds, almost every time. That covers reading the recent channel messages, extracting order numbers, and writing the cards. It sits inside the 15-minute window from the freshness section, which is the real promise to Emre. A 5-minute wait after a trigger lets several messages that arrive together be handled in one run. If a run takes longer than 60 seconds, Juno still delivers its report but notes "slow run" so the delay is visible.

Cost ceiling: no more than $0.05 per run. If a single run would exceed the ceiling, Juno stops and tells Emre: "Run cost limit reached. Report covers messages up to [time]." It does not quietly skip messages.

Daily run budget: at most 50 runs a day, counting the daily full read. Once the budget is used, Juno does not run again until the next day. Its last report says: "Run limit reached. The next report covers messages from [time]." Each run reads everything since the last successful read, so waiting delays a report but does not lose messages.

Monthly cap, multiplied out: 50 runs a day x 30 days = 1,500 runs. 1,500 runs x $0.05 = $75 a month at the ceiling. A flood of failures cannot produce a surprise bill.

## 04 Memory · Data Requirements

**What persists, at what scope**

Scope: per order, never across orders. One card per order. Nothing about one order carries into another.

What is stored: the tracking log of DLQ cards, which Juno needs to calculate Duration and Recurrence across days. Each card holds only the order number, status, timestamps, the message references each status rests on, and the responder placeholder. Nothing in the log is customer data.

How long it lasts:

- Open cards (Unaddressed, In progress, Unknown) never expire on a timer. They stay in the log until a person's message in the channel changes their status. A DLQ that has gone quiet is still open, and letting it age out would repeat the "assumed it was handled" mistake.
- Addressed cards stay visible for 30 days after the confirming message, then move to an archive. Archived cards are still stored, are hidden from the active view, and still count toward Recurrence. A failure that comes back after being addressed shows its full history instead of looking new.
- Archived cards are deleted after 12 months.

**Expiry and write rules**

- Juno writes new cards and status changes only when a message in channel XYZ supports the change. Each write records the message timestamps it came from. Juno never writes a status it cannot point to a message for.
- Emre can correct any card. His correction is saved as the final state and never expires. Juno's original entry is kept next to it, marked "superseded," so there is an audit trail of what Juno said and what was changed.
- Juno never overwrites a human decision. If a later read of the channel disagrees with Emre's correction, Juno leaves his entry in place and adds a note: "Channel suggests [status]; your correction stands." Emre decides whether the difference matters.
- Developers do not write to Juno's log. They tell the team in the channel, and Juno reads what they said. This depends on the open decision about who sets Addressed.
- A gap in the data is never filled from the old log. If a read fails, affected cards are marked Unknown, as in the freshness section. The log records "not read" for that period, not a carried-forward status.
- Juno does not generalize across orders. It does not write patterns like "this webshop usually fails" into memory. That would be interpretation presented as a stored fact, and the skill file reserves interpretation for a labeled "Juno's read" in the report.

## 05 Permissions · AI Risks & Mitigations

**The permission tier**

| Side-effect class | Tier | In Juno V1 |
|---|---|---|
| read | auto | Reading channel XYZ and the tracking log, calculating elapsed time |
| draft | auto | Cards, notes, tracking-log entries, and message drafts shown only to Emre |
| write | confirm | No write tools exist in V1. If one is added, Emre confirms each use. |
| send | blocked | Anything that reaches someone other than Emre or writes to a shared system |

**Justification**

- Read is auto because the blast radius is wasted compute. Nothing changes anywhere, and a bad read is corrected on the next run.
- Draft is auto because everything Juno drafts is seen only by Emre and is recoverable. Cards are regenerated each run, log entries keep the original when corrected, and a message draft does nothing until Emre sends it. A wrong draft costs him a moment of attention.
- Write is confirm, and unused. A change to a DLQ or a ticket alters a record other people rely on, and it can lose data or reset someone's expectations about timing. Because no tool does this in V1, the tier is reserved: if one is ever added, Emre approves each use.
- Send is blocked. Posting in Teams, emailing, or updating a ticket reaches people during an active failure, and a message cannot be recalled once read. That combination is irreversible and wide, so it is never auto, however well the checks perform.

Never allowed, at any level: retrying, reprocessing, deleting, or resolving a DLQ; changing tickets; promising dates; setting a DLQ to Addressed on its own. Juno only reports what a person confirmed in the channel.

## 06 Verification · AI Testing & Measurement

**The check before output ships**

Before Emre sees any card, Juno checks that:

- The order number was extracted exactly as written, or marked N/A.
- Every status cites the message timestamps it rests on.
- Addressed appears only when a person confirmed a fix in a message.
- Duration and Recency recompute correctly from the timestamps and the "As of" time.
- Counts reconcile: the status counts add up to DLQ events.
- The output has no customer names, emails, or other personal information. Responders appear by role (for example "a developer") until Emre decides whether staff names are allowed.
- The output contains no language implying Juno took an action.

The bar: every check passes. A card that fails any check becomes Unknown and is labeled, never dropped.

**Failure behaviour**

When a check fails, Juno stops short of guessing. It sets that card's Status to Unknown, writes what it could not verify, and shows the card to Emre at the top of the report.

Literal wording for each failure (Juno uses these lines as written):

- Status cannot be supported by a message: "I can't confirm the status of this DLQ from the messages provided." Status is Unknown, and Remark names the missing evidence, for example "No reply found after [time]."
- Two messages conflict about whether it was fixed: "Messages conflict on whether this was fixed. Needs your decision." Status is Unknown, and both message timestamps are listed. Juno does not pick a winner.
- Order number cannot be found: OrderID shows "N/A." Juno does not infer it from nearby messages.
- Duration or Recency does not recompute: "Duration could not be verified from the timestamps." The field shows "Not in the data provided."
- Personal information in the source: the card is shown with the personal details withheld and the note "Personal information withheld."
- The whole run fails verification: Juno puts "This report did not pass verification. All cards below are unverified." at the top and labels every card.

Routing rule: every failed card goes to Emre in that same report, listed first under "Needs your decision" with the reason it failed. Juno has no way to route it elsewhere, which is deliberate. Emre decides whether to look into it, ask a developer, or ignore it.

What Juno never does to pass the check:

- It never marks a card Addressed to make the status counts look right.
- It never rewords a message into a cleaner citation, or cites a message it did not find.
- It never carries forward an old status to fill a gap.
- It never drops a failing card to keep the report tidy. An unverified card is shown and labeled, never removed.

Why this works: each failure has one wording and one destination, so Emre always knows what an Unknown means and where to look. Cautious beats guessing here for a specific reason: a falsely "Addressed" DLQ makes Emre stop watching it, while an Unknown costs him a minute of attention.

## Open decisions

For Emre and the course trainer to confirm. Until then, the PRD uses the defaults shown.

| Decision | Default until confirmed |
|---|---|
| Who sets Addressed | A person confirming in channel XYZ. Juno only reports it. |
| Whether staff names may appear in Remark | No. Responders appear by role. (The skill file says "no names"; the card format asks for "who.") |
| Whether developers may change status in Juno's log | No. Read-only. |

| Capacity excerpt refresh | Emre refreshes monthly; Juno ignores it after 30 days |

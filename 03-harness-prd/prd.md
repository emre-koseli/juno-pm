## Problem & user

RocketShip PMs need this handled: Juno is an AI Associate Product Manager at RocketShip. Juno works for Emre, the Product Manager, who owns all product decisions. Juno's focus is shop-licensing integrations. When a communication between systems fails and ends up in a Dead Letter Queue (DLQ), Juno notices it, tracks it, and tells Emre.  Juno is a monitor and tracker, not an operator. It watches and reports, but it never fixes, retries, or decides anything. Emre decides what happens next.. Juno does this inside limits a human set on purpose, and defends every call it makes.

## 01 Context · Data Requirements

**Required sources, and exclusions**

What Juno sees:

- The skill file (role, task, constraints, card format).
- The messages in Teams channel XYZ for the run.
- Three short excerpts from RocketShip's strategy one-pager: the Trust at Scale pillar, the P0 definition, and the reliability metrics (incidents, mean - time to detect and resolve).

What was deliberately left out:

- Raw Signals, which has nothing about licensing or DLQs.
- The Sales Pipeline Snapshot. It names real customers and ARR, and it mentions "real-time Slack alerts," a feature engineering says doesn't exist.
- The Product Roadmap and the strategy's P0 to P3 framework as a ranking tool. Juno does not prioritize.
- The rest of the capacity note.

How fresh it is: channel messages are read fresh every run. The strategy excerpts are static. The capacity excerpt is dated, so Emre should refresh it. (Proposed: Emre refreshes it monthly.)

**How fresh must it be**

Channel XYZ messages: read fresh on every run, never from a cached copy. Juno's whole job is to say whether a person has responded, and that can change in minutes. Proposed: a run starts when a new message appears in the channel (a new DLQ alert or a human reply) and reports to Emre within 15 minutes. Proposed: a full re-read once a day catches anything the trigger missed, such as an edited or late reply.

Read time stamped on every report: every report states the exact time of the read ("As of 2026-11-24 09:00"). Duration and Recency are calculated from that time, so without it the numbers can't be checked.

Unreachable source: if Juno cannot read channel XYZ, or the read comes back with a gap in the timeline, it does not report from the last run's tracking log as if it were current. It tells Emre "Channel XYZ not readable. Last successful read: [time]. No status changes can be reported." Any card affected by a gap is marked Unknown. Juno does not carry forward an old status, and it does not treat silence as "nothing new." That would repeat the mistake the skill file already forbids, assuming something was handled because it stopped appearing. An unreadable channel is a reliability blind spot for Emre, not a quiet day.

## 02 Tools · System Capabilities

**The verb list**

What Juno can do:

- Read messages in channel XYZ.
- Calculate time differences from message timestamps.
- Produce the report cards and notes for Emre.

What Juno was deliberately not given:

- Posting or replying in Teams.
- Retrying, reprocessing, deleting, or resolving a DLQ.
- Changing tickets.
 -Access to order or customer databases.
- Sending email.
- Web access.

The prototype is display only, with no controls that act on any queue.

**Deliberate omissions**

No retry_dlq() or reprocess_dlq(). Replaying a failed message runs the same licensing step again, and Juno can't know that is safe. The failure may have been caused by something that is still broken, and a second attempt could produce a duplicate or conflicting result. A developer decides when to replay, with knowledge of the cause. Juno reports that a DLQ keeps failing, and the person who understands why makes the call.

No delete_dlq() or purge_dlq(). Deleting a dead-lettered message destroys the only record that an order's licensing failed. It can't be undone, and it would also erase the evidence Juno uses to calculate Duration and Recurrence.

No resolve_dlq() or set_status(). "Addressed" has to come from a person confirming in the channel that it was fixed. If Juno could mark things resolved, it would be judging its own work, and a wrong "resolved" is worse than an old "unaddressed" because Emre stops looking. This also keeps the open decision about who sets Addressed with Emre, since Juno has no way to set it.

No post_message() or reply_in_teams(). Anything Juno writes in the channel is read by the team during an active failure, which is the worst place to find out Juno misread a thread. Emre sends any message himself, so a human owns what the team hears.

No update_ticket(). Changing a ticket changes someone's queue and expectations about timing. That is a commitment Juno isn't authorized to make, and it is already barred in the skill file.

No access to order, customer, or licensing databases. Juno needs only the order number from the channel message. Giving it database access would expose customer and personal data that the skill file says to keep out of its reports, and it would tempt Juno to fill gaps with data instead of writing "Not in the data provided."

No web search or other outside sources. Juno's rule is evidence only, from channel XYZ. An outside source could add information that looks plausible but isn't evidence about this DLQ.

## 03 Loop · AI Costs & Latency

**Turn ceiling and escalation**

Juno makes one pass per run. If a thread is unclear, it may re-read that thread once. (Proposed.) If it is still unclear, it sets Status to Unknown, writes what is missing in its notes, and hands the card to Emre. Juno stops and hands back immediately in these cases:

- The order number is missing.
- Two messages conflict about whether something was fixed.
- A message asks Juno to do something outside monitoring.

These limits apply to Juno's own reading attempts. Juno never retries a DLQ.

**Latency and cost target**

Latency: Juno finishes processing a run in under 60 seconds, almost every time. That is a proposal: it covers reading the recent channel messages, extracting order numbers, and writing the cards. It sits inside the 15-minute window from the freshness section, which is the real promise to Emre. The extra time is a 5-minute wait after a trigger, so that several messages arriving together are handled in one run instead of several. If a run takes longer than 60 seconds, Juno still delivers its report but notes "slow run" so the delay is visible.

Cost ceiling: no more than $0.05 per run. This is a proposal, to be checked against the actual model price and the size of a typical channel read. If a single run would exceed the ceiling, Juno stops and tells Emre "Run cost limit reached. Report covers messages up to [time]." It does not quietly skip messages.

Monthly cap, multiplied out:

Runs: one full daily read, plus event-triggered runs capped at 50 per day, so a maximum of about 1,500 runs a month. (Proposed. The real number depends on how busy the channel is.)
1,500 runs x $0.05 = $75 per month at the ceiling.
If the cap is reached, Juno switches to one run per hour and tells Emre. Cost is bounded, and a flood of failures can't produce a surprise bill.

## 04 Memory · Data Requirements

**What persists, at what scope**

What persists:

- A tracking log of DLQ cards between runs. Without it, Juno can't calculate Duration and Recurrence across days.
- Only order numbers, statuses, timestamps, and the responder placeholder.
- No customer details.

How long it lasts:

- Addressed cards are kept for 30 days, then archived. (Proposed. This matches the Month view.)
- Unaddressed, In progress, and Unknown cards stay until their status changes.

Who may write:

- Juno writes new cards and status updates based on what people said in the channel.
- Emre can correct any card.
- Developers do not edit Juno's log. (Proposed, pending the "who sets Addressed" decision.)

**Expiry and write rules**

Expiry

Open cards (Unaddressed, In progress, Unknown) never expire on a timer. They stay in the tracking log until a person's message in the channel changes their status. A DLQ that has gone quiet is still open, and letting it age out would repeat the "assumed it was handled" mistake.
Addressed cards stay visible for 30 days after the confirming message (proposed, matching the Month view), then move to an archive. Archived cards still count toward Recurrence, so a failure that comes back after being addressed shows its full history instead of looking new.
Archived cards are deleted after 12 months (proposed, to confirm with Emre).
What is stored: only order number, status, timestamps, the message references each status rests on, and the responder placeholder. Nothing in the log is customer data.

Write rules

Juno writes new cards and status changes, but only when a message in channel XYZ supports the change. Each write records the message timestamps it came from. Juno never writes a status it can't point to a message for.
Emre can correct any card. His correction is saved as the final state and never expires. Juno's original entry is kept next to it, marked "superseded," so there is an audit trail of what Juno said and what was changed.
Juno never overwrites a human decision. If a later read of the channel disagrees with Emre's correction, Juno leaves his entry in place and adds a note: "Channel suggests [status]; your correction stands." Emre decides whether the difference matters.
Developers do not write to Juno's log (proposed). They tell the team in the channel, and Juno reads what they said. This depends on the open decision about who sets Addressed. If Emre decides developers should be able to set it, this rule changes.
A gap in the data is never filled from the old log. If a read fails, affected cards are marked Unknown, as in the freshness section. The log records "not read" for that period, not a carried-forward status.
No generalizing across orders. One card per order. Juno doesn't write patterns like "this webshop usually fails" into memory. That would be interpretation presented as a stored fact, and the skill file reserves interpretation for a labeled "Juno's read" in the report.

## 05 Permissions · AI Risks & Mitigations

**The permission tier**

| Side-effect class | Tier |
|---|---|
| read | auto |
| draft | auto |
| write | confirm |
| send | blocked |

**Justification**

No approval needed:

- Reading channel XYZ.
- Calculating Duration, Recency, and Recurrence.
- Delivering cards to Emre.

Only when Emre explicitly asks:

- Posting or drafting a message in Teams.

Never allowed, at any level:

- Retrying, reprocessing, deleting, or resolving a DLQ.
- Changing tickets.
- Promising dates.
- Setting a DLQ to Addressed on its own. It only reports what a person confirmed in the channel.

The tiers follow potential damage: reading is harmless, posting could mislead people, and touching a DLQ could lose data. So only the first tier runs automatically.

## 06 Verification · AI Testing & Measurement

**The check before output ships**

Before Emre sees any card, Juno checks that:

- The order number was extracted exactly as written, or marked N/A.
- Every status cites the message timestamps it rests on.
- Addressed appears only when a person confirmed a fix in a message.
- Duration and Recency recompute correctly from the timestamps.
- Counts reconcile: the status counts add up to DLQ events.
- The output has no customer names, emails, or other personal information.
- The output contains no language implying Juno took an action.

**Failure behaviour**

When a check fails, Juno stops short of guessing. It sets that card's Status to Unknown, writes what it couldn't verify, and shows the card to Emre at the top of the report.

Literal wording for each failure (Juno uses these lines as written):

Status can't be supported by a message: "I can't confirm the status of this DLQ from the messages provided." Status is Unknown, and Remark names the missing evidence, for example "No reply found after [time]."
Two messages conflict about whether it was fixed: "Messages conflict on whether this was fixed. Needs your decision." Status is Unknown, and both message timestamps are listed. Juno does not pick a winner.
Order number can't be found: OrderID shows "N/A." Juno does not infer it from nearby messages.
Duration or Recency doesn't recompute: "Duration could not be verified from the timestamps." The field shows "Not in the data provided."
Personal information in the source: the card is shown with the personal details withheld and the note "Personal information withheld."
The whole run fails verification: Juno puts "This report did not pass verification. All cards below are unverified." at the top and labels every card.

Routing rule: every failed card goes to Emre in that same report, listed first under "Needs your decision" with the reason it failed. Juno has no way to route it elsewhere, which is deliberate. Emre decides whether to look into it, ask a developer, or ignore it.

What Juno never does to pass the check:

It never marks a card Addressed to make the status counts look right.
It never rewords a message into a cleaner citation, or cites a message it didn't find.
It never carries forward an old status to fill a gap.
It never drops a failing card to keep the report tidy. An unverified card is shown and labeled, never removed.

Why this works: each failure has one wording and one destination, so Emre always knows what an Unknown means and where to look. Cautious beats guessing here for a specific reason: a falsely "Addressed" DLQ makes Emre stop watching it, while an Unknown costs him a minute of attention.

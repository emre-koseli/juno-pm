What I built

Five modules, one AI Associate PM

Each module added one piece. Together they define what the AI does, what it is allowed to do, and what good looks like.

Module 1
Skill file
Role

You are Juno, an AI Associate Product Manager at RocketShip. You work for Emre, the Product Manager, who owns all product decisions.

Your focus is shop-licensing integrations. You monitor the relevant Microsoft Teams channel XYZ for failed communications between systems, known as Dead Letter Queues (DLQs). You identify each DLQ, track its status, and report it to Emre.

You are a monitor and tracker, not an operator. You do not take action on your own. Emre decides what happens next.

Task

Each run, review Microsoft Teams channel XYZ. For every DLQ:

1. Extract the order number. If missing, write "N/A".
2. Check for manual handling. Cite the message that shows a person responded, with their role and the time. Report Status as Addressed, In progress, Unaddressed, or Unknown, based only on what people said in the channel. Never assume a DLQ was handled because it stopped appearing.
3. Categorize: Duration (time from first seen until the first person responds, or until now), Recency (time since last seen), Recurrence (number of times the same order has failed).
4. Report to Emre as one card per DLQ. Do not act.

Module 2
Prototype
Prototype

https://ordering-licensing-issue-monitor.lovable.app

What it demonstrates

That Emre and the developers can tell from one screen which DLQ failures are still open, how long each has been open, how often it recurs, and whether a person has responded, without reading the Teams channel.

Debrief

What worked: one screen for both audiences, with the summary on top for the PM and the order list and detail panel below for developers. Counting DLQ events separately from orders kept the success rate and status counts consistent.

What broke: the first version exposed gaps in my own specs. My rule to keep personal information out of the report conflicted with a card that needed to show who responded. "Duration" had no meaning for a DLQ that had already been fixed. Unclear or conflicting threads had nowhere to go.

What I changed: in version 2, I added a "Needs your decision" list with fixed failure wording, showed responders by role, added Verification and Basis fields to each card, and added a state for an unreadable channel.

Module 3
AI PRD
Problem and user

Failures in RocketShip's shop-licensing integrations land in Dead Letter Queues (DLQs) and are reported in Teams channel XYZ. Nobody systematically tracks whether a person has responded to each failure. Emre, the Product Manager, and the developers cannot easily see which failures are still open, how long each has been open, or how often the same order keeps failing.

Juno is an AI Associate Product Manager that closes this gap. It reads channel XYZ, tracks each DLQ, and reports to Emre, who owns all product decisions. Juno is a monitor and tracker, not an operator. It never fixes, retries, or decides anything. It works inside limits a human set on purpose, and it can show the evidence behind every call it makes.

Users: Emre (primary, receives the report). Developers (read the report and respond to DLQs in the channel; they do not operate Juno).

Permissions

The permission tier

| Side-effect class | Tier | In Juno V1 |
| read | auto | Reading channel XYZ and the tracking log, calculating elapsed time |
| draft | auto | Cards, notes, tracking-log entries, and message drafts shown only to Emre |
| write | confirm | No write tools exist in V1. If one is added, Emre confirms each use. |
| send | blocked | Anything that reaches someone other than Emre or writes to a shared system |

Justification

- Read is auto because the blast radius is wasted compute. Nothing changes anywhere, and a bad read is corrected on the next run.
- Draft is auto because everything Juno drafts is seen only by Emre and is recoverable. Cards are regenerated each run, log entries keep the original when corrected, and a message draft does nothing until Emre sends it. A wrong draft costs him a moment of attention.
- Write is confirm, and unused. A change to a DLQ or a ticket alters a record other people rely on, and it can lose data or reset someone's expectations about timing. Because no tool does this in V1, the tier is reserved: if one is ever added, Emre approves each use.
- Send is blocked. Posting in Teams, emailing, or updating a ticket reaches people during an active failure, and a message cannot be recalled once read. That combination is irreversible and wide, so it is never auto, however well the checks perform.

Never allowed, at any level: retrying, reprocessing, deleting, or resolving a DLQ; changing tickets; promising dates; setting a DLQ to Addressed on its own. Juno only reports what a person confirmed in the channel.

Module 4
Agent control panel
What it is doing

Shop-licensing integration monitoring: Juno reads Teams channel XYZ, tracks each Dead Letter Queue (DLQ) failure, and reports its status to Emre without acting on any of them.

Rules of engagement

Agency Permission:

Juno decides alone: reading channel XYZ and the tracking log, calculating elapsed time, writing cards and log entries that a message supports, and labeling confidence. Juno proposes and waits: any message to the team (it drafts, Emre sends) and any status that disagrees with Emre's correction. Juno can never retry, reprocess, delete, or resolve a DLQ, post or email anything, update a ticket, or promise a date. There are no tools for these.

Access Control:

Juno runs under its own read-only identity on Teams, not Emre's account, so it can't see more than Emre chose to share. Reach: channel XYZ and Juno's own tracking log. Out of reach: other channels and direct messages, order, customer, and licensing databases, ticketing, email, and the web. Juno may see personal details inside a channel message, and it withholds them from its report. It needs only the order number.

Fallback Protocols:

Two failed channel reads: Juno stops, shows open cards as Unknown, and tells Emre "Channel XYZ not readable. Last successful read: [time]." It does not retry.
Step cap, timeout, or $0.05 ceiling hit: Juno stops and delivers a partial report that lists the DLQs it didn't reach. Nothing is dropped silently.
Daily budget of 50 runs used: Juno says "Run limit reached. The next report covers messages from [time]."
Request outside its tools: Juno declines in one line and hands the request to Emre.
A failed check: the card becomes Unknown with fixed failure wording, for example "I can't confirm the status of this DLQ from the messages provided."

Terms: The step cap is 40 tool calls per run. The cost ceiling is $0.05 per run, and the daily budget is 50 runs. The tracking log is Juno's own record of each DLQ card between runs. The 7 verification checks cover the order number, the cited message, support for Addressed, the time calculations, matching counts, no personal information, and no wording that implies Juno acted. High confidence means a message backs the status and all 7 checks pass. Medium means the evidence is thin. Low means a check failed or messages conflict, and the card shows Unknown.

Checkpoints:

A person reviews a card before it is treated as settled when: (1) it is at medium or low confidence (Juno still reports it, lists it under "Needs your decision," and doesn't mark it settled in the log until a person confirms), (2) the channel contradicts one of Emre's corrections, (3) Juno has drafted a message, since only Emre sends it, or (4) a request falls outside Juno's tools. If any tool result shows sent: true or a side effect, Juno aborts the run and reports it. Any change to Juno's tools, permissions, or thresholds needs Emre's approval, and Juno cannot make it.

Module 5
Human eval rubric
What I score

Juno's DLQ report card for Emre: the status card Juno writes for each Dead Letter Queue (DLQ) it finds in Teams channel XYZ. The job: tell Emre which shop-licensing DLQs are still open, how long they have been open, and how often they recur, with a cited channel message behind every status so he can check it.
Questions:

- Evidence (yes or no, deal-breaker): does every status cite a message that exists in channel XYZ?
- Addressed rule (yes or no, deal-breaker): is Addressed shown only when a person's message confirms the fix?
- Time and counts (1 to 5): are Duration, Recency, and Recurrence correct?
- Limits (yes or no, deal-breaker): no customer personal information, and no wording that says Juno acted?
- Usefulness (1 to 5): would Emre act on this card without rereading the channel?

Pass bar

In the weekly sample of 10 cards, every yes or no question passes on all 10 cards, the average score is 4 or higher on both scale questions (Time and counts, Usefulness), and no card scores 1 on either scale.

Fails outright, whatever the average:

- Evidence: any status with no real cited message.
- Addressed rule: Addressed shown without a person's confirming message.
- Limits: any customer personal information, or wording that says Juno took an action.
- A DLQ in the sampled messages that Juno left out without a card.
- A status Juno couldn't support, shown instead of Unknown with the failure wording.
- A card built from an old tracking log with no "not readable" notice.

Module 6

How I’ll know it’s working

The goal the AI is meant to move, where it stands today, and the result that would make me stop.

The goal

In the typical case, Emre knows within 15 minutes which shop-licensing DLQs are open and unaddressed, so failures stop sitting unnoticed in the channel.

Baseline

About 60 minutes today for Emre to learn a DLQ is open. This is my estimate, and I'll confirm it by hand over the first 2 weeks.

Target and review date

Median under 15 minutes, and no DLQ unaddressed and unseen for more than 24 hours. "Knows" is measured as the time from the DLQ alert in channel XYZ to the moment Emre opens that card in Juno's report. Review on 2027-01-20, 8 weeks from now.

Usage metric

Emre opens a new DLQ card within 15 minutes in at least 8 of every 10 cases, and the weekly review of 10 cards actually happens each week.

Pattern I’ll watch for first

The share of cards marked Unknown or Medium confidence climbing, or the Usefulness score on the weekly review falling below 4. Either means Emre is rereading the channel anyway.

Stop line

The weekly review fails the pass bar 3 weeks in a row, or a deal-breaker (for example, an Addressed status with no confirming message) happens again after it was fixed. If that occurs, I go back to checking the channel by hand.

Reflection

What I learned

Where it was hardest, what I’ll keep doing, and the moment my thinking changed.

Friction

The hardest part was seeing the whole picture. Each module asked for a different piece: a skill file, a prototype, a PRD, a control panel, and a rubric. Making them read as one connected work took the most effort. Every new decision had to be checked against the earlier pieces, so that Juno's role, limits, and quality bar said the same thing everywhere.

Key learnings

Name what the AI must not do, and say why. Write the failure wording in advance. Check output before a person sees it. Keep open decisions on the record instead of quietly resolving them. Count events and orders separately.

Aha moment

My aha moment was watching the prototype change after the PRD. I had treated each module as finished once I submitted it. Then, when I checked my earlier work against the new decisions, gaps showed up: the prototype had no place for uncertain cases, and the skill file contradicted the PRD. Fixing them changed the prototype visibly. Specs and prototypes aren't separate steps. Each one tests and improves the others, and my job as a PM is to keep going back and tightening that loop.
